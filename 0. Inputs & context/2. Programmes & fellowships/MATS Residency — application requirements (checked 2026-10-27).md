## What it is

A hybrid research residency (London, Berkeley or Washington, DC; at least a third of the time in person; residents can move between offices). It is separate from the main MATS programme. There are three tracks:

- **Bridge**: the one that fits her. It is for experienced researchers and professionals moving into AI safety from an adjacent field, and it names people with "deep expertise in policy and governance and diplomacy" and compute governance among the domains it wants. It lasts 6 to 24 months in 12-week blocks. Newcomers start with a Safety Orientation Week and a three-week Translation Sprint, in which they take a piece of prior work and reframe it around a safety-relevant threat model. A Placement Desk connects Bridge residents with employers. "For recent PhD graduates, the Bridge track offers an alternative to a conventional postdoctoral position" (saved PDF).
- **Anchor**: 1 to 2 years as an independent staff researcher, for people with a track record of open-ended independent work.
- **Incubation**: 6 to 24 months to found a new research agenda or organisation.

At the end of each 12-week block, residents submit a "Milestone Package" (work done, evidence produced, next objectives), reviewed on "reviewable evidence and demonstrated progress, not written updates alone".

## Eligibility

- Open internationally, with visa support.
- No PhD, publication record or AI safety background required; applicants are judged on "the quality and seriousness of your work".
- The website doesn't say whether current PhD students or part-time residents are possible. Ask residency@matsprogram.org before applying.

## What the application asks for

**For every track**

1. Profile: contact details, current affiliation, **CV (2 pages maximum)**, office preference, which intake she is available for, work authorisation, highest degree, previous MATS involvement. Not assessed.
2. Track choice, with **two or three sentences on why it fits**.
3. **Two referees** who can speak to the quality of her work.
4. **One representative work sample**: a paper, repository, technical report, evaluation or policy analysis.
5. **Two short written responses** (wording and limits not published), plus a **counterfactual question**: what she would do if not offered a place.
6. **AI-use disclosure**: whether and how AI tools were used, and agreement with their AI-use policy.

**Bridge track, in addition**

7. **A research plan with a theory of change.**
8. **"Why Bridge?"**: why the Residency is right for her now.
9. **How her previous work prepares her for AI safety research.**
