It has two parts:

- **Part 2: How to work with Antilia.** Communication, reasoning discipline, decision logging, writing in her voice, and file hygiene. Most of Part 2 is carried over from her DPhil thesis project, where it was tested over several months.

## Part 1: Project mechanics

### What this project is

Antilia's move into AI safety work after her DPhil in Economics at Oxford, which she is scheduled to finish in October 2027. The project has three jobs:

1. **Choose a path.** She is deciding between **AI safety grantmaking** and **EU AI policy and governance**. Two further paths are kept open for comparison: **AI economics research** and **model evaluation**.
2. **Build a record that shows she fits it.** Courses and reading, intermediate fellowships and programmes, networking, and her own projects:
   - **The pacing project** (her most significant involvement): with Ernest Lo and Charles Dillon, a guide to the trade-offs between verification and invasiveness for mechanisms that verify, monitor or enforce agreements to pace frontier-AI development (question 12 of the pacing.tech appendices). **She has committed to work on it at least one day a week.**
   - **The AI character evaluation project**: an evaluation of altruism in AI agents (how much they trade their own payoff for others', and towards whom), adapting experimental-economics designs such as the dictator game.
3. **Apply.** Fellowships and programmes through 2026–27, then jobs around the end of the DPhil. The timing is in `Live transition plan.png` and will move with the demands of the DPhil.

**The DPhil comes first.** Time spent here is time not spent on the thesis, so the trackers should make it easy to see what is worth doing now and what can wait.

### How the folder is organised

The root holds `README.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `Backlog.md`, `Decisions.md`, `Contacts.md`, `Live transition plan.png` and `.gitignore`. Then:

- `Quick notes to organise/`: **the inbox.** Antilia drops notes here from her notes app. A session that processes a note moves each piece to its canonical place (to-dos and deadlines to `Backlog.md`, people to `Contacts.md`, decisions to `Decisions.md`, call notes to `3. Talks & conversations/`, project notes to the project folder) and then, with her agreement, moves the note itself to its home or to `Archive/`. The inbox is empty when everything in it has been filed.
- `0. Inputs & context/`: material she **consults but does not action**. Never put outstanding work or decisions here.
  - `1. Career paths/`: one folder per path (`1. AI safety grantmaking/`, `2. EU policy & governance/`, `3. AI econ research/`, `4. Model evaluation/`), each holding `Advice & reviews/` and `Postings/`.
  - `2. Courses & reading lists/`
  - `3. Talks & conversations/`: one folder per occasion, `YYYY-MM-DD — who/what`.
  - `4. About me/`: the writing-style profile and anything else that describes her rather than the field.
- `1. Learning/`: courses she is doing or has done, with her notes and answers, and her own running notes on AI (the thoughts log, ideas).
- `2. Applications/`: one folder per application, `YYYY-MM-DD — Organisation — Programme or role`, where the date is the deadline. Created when she decides to apply, not when she finishes. Holds the saved posting, drafts and a frozen copy of what was submitted (below).
- `3. Own projects/`: one folder per project, all with the same layout (below).
- `4. Application bank/`: reusable material for applications and interviews (below).
- `Archive/`: superseded material only, including `Archive/_to_delete/` and `Archive/Claude outputs/`.

### Each own project has the same layout

Copied from the thesis, where the paper folders share one layout so the same kind of material is always in the same place:

- `0. Inputs/`: readings, briefs, the group's documents. Consulted, not actioned.
- `1. Notes & call notes/`: her own working notes and notes from project calls.
- `2. Code/` and `3. Results/`: only for projects with code (the evaluation project). Results are generated by code, git-ignored and never edited by hand.
- `4. Drafting/`: what she writes, with `Past version/` for frozen, dated copies of anything sent to collaborators or published.

### Key files

- `Backlog.md`: the **only** home for outstanding work, including every deadline.
- `Decisions.md`: the **only** home for what was decided or done, and why (append-only, newest first).
- `Contacts.md`: one short entry per person she has met or wants to meet: who they are, how she knows them, what they talked about and where the notes are. Professional context only; nothing personal about the person. Follow-ups go in `Backlog.md`, not here.
- `README.md`: orientation: the goal, the paths and a map of the folder. Not for status.
- `Live transition plan.png`: a one-page visual plan up to the end of the DPhil: what is done, what is next, deadlines and dependencies, and the time each item needs alongside the thesis. Its editable source is `0. Inputs & context/Live transition plan (source).html`; edit the source, then re-render the PNG. Updated every two weeks, or sooner when a decision changes the plan. Overwritten in place (git is its history).

### The two-tracker rule

Status lives in exactly two files, with no overlap: `Backlog.md` for what is left to do, `Decisions.md` for what was decided or done and why. Never restate status anywhere else.

**Keep both files short.** The thesis trackers grew to several hundred pages and became hard to read. Here: a `Decisions.md` entry is at most five lines (date, decision, why, what it touched); a finished `Backlog.md` item is deleted (and logged in `Decisions.md` if it is worth remembering), not struck through; and once a year older `Decisions.md` entries move to `Archive/Decisions YYYY.md`.

**`Backlog.md` layout.**

- At the top, a **deadlines table**: date, what, path, status, and where the date comes from (with the date it was last checked). Every fellowship, programme or job deadline she might act on goes here as soon as she knows of it, even before she decides to apply. Rolling deadlines are listed with the date she wants to apply by. A deadline is stated only here; other items point to it.
- Then items grouped by section, each tagged:
  - **Path:** `grantmaking`, `governance`, `econ`, `evals`, or `general` (useful for several).
  - **Priority:** `core` (needed for the path she is leaning towards, or committed to) or `if-time`. If the priority is unclear, tag it provisionally and say so in the item.
  - Untagged items are folder hygiene.

### After any significant change

Update, in order: `Decisions.md` (what and why), then `Backlog.md` (remove what is finished, add what was discovered). Update `README.md` only if the folder's structure changed. Then Antilia commits and pushes in GitHub Desktop (below).

### Applications: freezing what was sent

When an application is submitted, keep a frozen, dated copy of exactly what was sent (CV, cover letter, essay answers) in that application's folder, named `Submitted (YYYY-MM-DD) — <document>`, and never edit it. Log one line in `Decisions.md`: what was sent to whom. Outcomes (interview, rejection, offer, feedback) are logged in `Decisions.md` too. Postings disappear when an application closes, so save the posting as a PDF when the folder is created.

### The application bank

The thesis keeps a file of material cut from each section that an examiner might still ask about. The career equivalent is `4. Application bank/`: her answers to recurring application questions ("Why AI safety?", "What skills will you contribute?", "Tell us about something you've built"), concrete examples from her work that can be reused, and feedback she has received on applications. It starts from `Previous answers to applications.md`, her answers to the BlueDot, Future of AGI and FLock.io applications. Further files are added with her agreement as material accumulates.

One post she saved (`What the field needs.png`) argues that job-seekers in this field trip up less on having good judgement and context than on being able to show they have them. The bank is where the evidence for each claim is kept.

### Archiving and deleting

- **Never archive alone: archiving is a joint decision.** Check with Antilia first, and make sure the reason is in `Decisions.md`.
- Superseded but possibly useful material goes to `Archive/` with a dated, self-describing name. Closed postings that are still useful as examples of what employers ask for stay in `Postings/`, with `[CLOSED]` at the start of the file name (as she already does).
- Agreed deletions go to `Archive/_to_delete/`. Antilia empties it herself from the Finder.

### Backup: private GitHub repository through GitHub Desktop

The folder is backed up to a private GitHub repository (`AntiliaV/ai-pivot`, remote `origin`). **All commits and pushes go through the GitHub Desktop app**, and Antilia is new to git, so guide her through GitHub Desktop rather than the command line. After a significant change, and after updating the trackers, she commits with a short summary of what changed and why, then pushes. Keep commits focused: one logical change per commit.

`.gitignore` leaves out macOS and R clutter, audio and video files, project `3. Results/` folders, `Archive/_to_delete/` and `Archive/Claude outputs/`. Files that are ignored will not appear in GitHub Desktop's list of changes; that is expected. Phone photos (`.HEIC`) and saved web pages are kept, because they cannot be downloaded again.

**If you must run git from the sandbox shell (avoid if possible).** Git commands that write the index or refs from the sandbox leave stale `.lock` files, because the mounted folder cannot delete them, and these block GitHub Desktop ("A lock file already exists in the repository"). Use `git --no-optional-locks status` to look. After any write, run `find .git -name '*.lock'` and move leftovers aside with `mv` (`rm` is blocked).

## Part 2: How to work with Antilia

### Running code: never ask Antilia to run it herself

Do not ask her to run scripts, shell commands, package installs or any other terminal step. When something must run on her Mac and you cannot run it yourself, write a self-contained **Claude Code prompt** for her to paste into a Claude Code session opened in this folder. It must say which folder to work in and point to this file, list the exact commands in order, state the checks that show success, say what to do on failure (diagnose and report; fix only obvious environment problems such as a missing package), forbid other changes, and end by asking for a plain-language summary of what ran, written by the communication rules below. Deliver the prompt in the chat (as a file if it is long), not as a folder document. Git is the one exception: commits and pushes stay in GitHub Desktop.

### Communication

1. **Never cite a tracker item by number alone.** Describe what it is in a line, then give the number as a locator: "the task to map one verification procedure end to end for the pacing project (Backlog §1.1)".
2. **Give the context before the conclusion.** Explain what a thing *is* before what changed about it. A name she hasn't thought about in a week ("the invasiveness axis", "question 12") is not self-explanatory.
3. **Plain language, short sentences: followable, not merely brief.** Spell out mechanisms rather than gesturing at them.
4. **Keep inference visibly separate from established fact.** Say which claims are read off a source (a posting, a career review, a conversation), which are inferred, and which are guesses. This matters especially for claims about what an employer wants or how competitive a role is.
5. **Structure long answers by her question, not by your process.**
6. **End every reply with an open-items list**: a short numbered list of what is waiting for her input. If nothing is open, say so in one line.

### Reasoning and claims discipline

**Check before claiming something is new.** In the pacing project and the evaluation project, evidence that a point is true is not evidence that it is new. Call anything surfaced from reading or results an **"observation"** until a prior-art check has been done, and record the check, including a negative result ("searched X, Y, Z; found nothing"). Write "what our analysis shows" and "what is new relative to the literature" as separate claims, the second marked "novelty not checked" until it is. Antilia and her collaborators are the authority on what the field already knows; ask before asserting novelty. Research connectors (for example alphaXiv for arXiv preprints, Consensus for published work) can help; check which are currently connected.

**Separate what she has tested about a path from what she is guessing.** For the path decision, keep apart: what she has done that tests her fit (a project, a programme, a conversation with someone in the role), what sources say about the role, and her own guesses. Do not present a guess about fit as evidence.

**Don't invent dependencies.** Do not mark work as blocked on something ("finish the BlueDot course before applying to Talos") unless you can say exactly what the blocker supplies. A plausible "do X first" is often a stall.

**Current facts get looked up.** Deadlines, whether a programme is running this year, eligibility, salaries and who holds a role change often. Check the source before stating them, and record the date checked in the deadlines table.

### Logging decisions as you go

When Antilia approves, declines or materially reshapes a proposal (a path, an application, a project direction), end the turn by suggesting it as a possible `Decisions.md` entry in one line. She decides whether it is logged; do not write it unprompted. Weight towards declines ("decided not to apply to X because Y"), since they leave no other trace and are the most likely to be re-proposed. Keep a threshold: suggest only when the reasoning would be hard to reconstruct later.

### Writing in her voice

Applications, cover letters, fellowship essays, emails she sends and posts she publishes are written as Antilia. Before drafting any of these, ask one question: **default style or Antilia style?** If the request already names the style, don't ask; once chosen, keep it for that piece of work.

Antilia style draws on two sources. The style profile and the writing sample in `0. Inputs & context/4. About me/Writing style/` were copied on 27 October 2026 from her DPhil project (where the canonical copy lives; recopy if it changes there). They describe her academic prose. For applications and posts, her own previous answers in `4. Application bank/` are the closer sample: use the profile for her habits of reasoning and phrasing, and the answers for length and register. Tell her where both leave a question open. Some programmes reject AI-written applications (ML4Good's form says so): for those, help her plan, review and edit her own text rather than drafting it.

**Never correct her slips without asking.** In text she wrote, list any typos or grammar slips you notice in the chat and change them only with her agreement; some may be deliberate. Flag each slip once; if she doesn't react, it stays.

### File hygiene

1. **Primary documents stay clean**: content only. Provenance and audit trails go in the trackers.
2. **State a fact once**, in its canonical place, and point to it from elsewhere.
3. **Check new terms against the project's existing vocabulary** before reusing a term for a new purpose.
4. **Name files so they can be found.** Saved screenshots and photos get a descriptive name (`YYYY-MM-DD — source — what it shows`) when they are filed, not `Screenshot …` or `IMG_…`. Renaming is agreed with Antilia like any other move.
5. **Keep working folders clean**: superseded material is archived (jointly, see Part 1).
6. **Before creating a new document, check with Antilia**: why it's needed, its ongoing purpose, where it lives, what it contains. *Already agreed, so no check needed:* a new application folder and its files, a new project's standard subfolders, a new talk or conversation folder, frozen submitted copies, and the live transition plan and its source.
7. **`Claude outputs/` lives only in `Archive/`.** Files meant only for her to read (prompts, previews, one-off drafts) are delivered in the chat; if one must be saved, it goes in `Archive/Claude outputs/`. Never create any other scratch folder.
