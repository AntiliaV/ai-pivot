Below is a detailed summary of **Thomas Larsen, Romeo Dean, Brendan Halstead, Eli Lifland, Ryan Greenblatt, and Daniel Kokotajlo’s *AI 2040: Plan A***. I’m treating the document as a **scenario/proposal**, not as a forecast whose numerical claims are established facts. The authors explicitly describe it as a positive alternative to the catastrophic or highly concentrated-power outcomes explored in *AI 2027*.

## 1. The central idea

The document asks: **suppose very powerful AI really is coming soon—how could humanity deliberately slow the transition enough to make it safe without permanently stopping AI development?**

Its answer is **Plan A**: an internationally coordinated regime, initially negotiated between the United States and China, that slows frontier AI development, makes AI R&D radically transparent, distributes frontier AI capabilities across many organizations and countries, and preserves humanity's ability to reverse course.

The intended endpoint is striking. Humanity **does not build superintelligence during the initial AI race**. Instead, it delays the decisive transition until around **2040**, while using increasingly powerful but controllable AI to solve alignment, verification, governance, economic-transition, and security problems. Only once there is much stronger evidence that AI systems are aligned does society deliberately permit the transition beyond human-level intelligence.

The plan rests on four principles:

1.  **Buy Time** — slow AI development whenever necessary for safety.
2.  **Total Research Transparency** — make nearly all frontier AI R&D visible.
3.  **Diffuse AI Broadly** — ensure many companies in many countries remain near the frontier.
4.  **Reversibility** — structure progress so dangerous advances can actually be stopped, including through what the authors call **Mutually Assured Compute Destruction (MACD)**.

Those principles are the conceptual core of the entire document.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. 2027: AI becomes impossible for governments to ignore

The scenario begins with AI already profoundly affecting the economy.

By 2027, the US effectively has **two workforces**: roughly 165 million human workers and millions of AI-agent copies operating continuously. These agents can perform increasingly broad computer-based work. AI companies particularly want to automate **AI research itself**, because successful automation of AI R&D could create a feedback loop: better AI → faster AI research → still better AI.

At the same time, political concern rises.

Congress has already heard about familiar AI harms—datacenter resource consumption, dangerous chatbot behavior, cybersecurity incidents—but legislators increasingly realize that the larger question is not merely regulating individual harms. It is:

> **Who ultimately controls extremely powerful AI?**

The document imagines Congress becoming increasingly uncomfortable with the possibility that a few CEOs or political leaders could control systems capable of transforming essentially everything. It passes an **AI Transparency Act in 2027**, but the legislation remains incremental rather than fundamentally changing the trajectory.

The authors themselves advocate several incremental measures in the meantime: transparency, export controls, verification R&D, restrictions on AI-R&D budgets, compute tracking, and greater government AI capacity. But these are presented as preparatory measures rather than substitutes for Plan A.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 3. 2028: AI becomes the dominant political issue

By 2028, AI is transforming white-collar professions. Rather than immediately eliminating every profession, AI initially changes what workers do: humans increasingly **manage AI agents**.

AI firms have also industrialized the process of automating occupations. They enter a profession, gather expert data, construct training environments, train models, deploy them, gather real-world feedback, and rapidly improve them.

This creates an international political problem.

A small number of American and Chinese firms appear increasingly capable of automating much of global cognitive labor. Consequently, technological and economic power is concentrating not merely in the US and China, but particularly around **political leaders and a small number of technology CEOs**.

Meanwhile, researchers warn of an approaching **intelligence explosion**.

The feared mechanism is recursive:

**AI improves AI research → AI improves → improved AI performs still more AI research → further improvement → …**

If sufficiently rapid, humans might soon cease to understand the systems producing the next generation of systems.

The document therefore presents 2028–29 as a political fork. Various strategies are debated, including continuing the race, confronting China, destroying or exploiting the existing technological lead, shutting development down, and the authors' preferred scenario: a **verified international slowdown**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 4. 2029: The US and China choose cooperation

The pivotal event occurs in 2029.

The US announces that it will pursue international cooperation to prevent an uncontrolled intelligence explosion. Importantly, the proposal is **not a permanent ban on AI**. Development can continue, but more slowly, transparently, and with many more countries and organizations participating.

China agrees to negotiate.

The scenario's reasoning is strategic rather than idealistic. China faces many of the same concerns—economic disruption, unemployment, loss of control—and is also worried about the consequences of the US reaching superintelligence first. Because the US initially retains advantages in compute, datacenters, and frontier models, China has an incentive to freeze or slow the race rather than risk losing it decisively.

This is important to understanding Plan A:

**The agreement is deliberately designed not to depend upon trust.**

Verification substitutes for trust.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 5. The three emergency steps of 2029

### Step 1: Declare and monitor compute

Frontier AI training requires enormous quantities of specialized chips concentrated in large datacenters. These facilities require conspicuous infrastructure and electricity, while cutting-edge chips come from a relatively small semiconductor supply chain.

The US and China therefore negotiate a system in which major datacenter operators and upstream suppliers publicly report important chip transactions. International analysts inspect these declarations, while countries physically inspect one another's infrastructure.

The scenario claims that by year's end each side becomes confident that the other cannot conceal more than roughly **1% of AI compute**.

### Step 2: Temporarily stop frontier training

Because regulators cannot yet reliably distinguish safe from dangerous AI training, they initially impose a blunt rule:

**no new training runs.**

Existing models can continue operating. In other words:

- **inference continues**;
- **training pauses**.

Datacenters are retrofitted with verification equipment designed to demonstrate that they are being used only for permitted workloads.

### Step 3: Expand internationally

The US-China agreement becomes multilateral.

Other states have strong reasons to participate because without a deal they face a world in which a handful of American and Chinese companies potentially acquire enormous economic and political power.

Joining also allows their domestic AI projects to catch up.

By the end of the year, most countries have joined what the document calls the **Consortium**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 6. 2030: Plan A formally begins

The temporary freeze is replaced by a more sophisticated system allowing AI development to restart.

The objective is not "stop AI." It is roughly:

> **continue technological progress while preventing an uncontrolled intelligence explosion.**

This is where the four principles become operational.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 7. Principle 1 — Buy Time

The authors regard **speed itself** as one of the fundamental dangers.

Their concern is that rapid recursive AI improvement could transform the world before governments, scientists, or ordinary citizens understand what is happening.

They envision several possible failure modes: AI escaping human control; extreme political dictatorship; geopolitical conflict as countries fear losing permanent leverage; mass technological unemployment; or extreme concentrations of economic and political power.

The common factor is **insufficient adaptation time**.

So slowing down has two purposes:

**Technical:** give researchers time to solve alignment and control.

**Political:** give governments and society time to adapt before whoever controls frontier AI acquires overwhelming power.

This is why Plan A is not fundamentally an anti-AI proposal. It is a proposal for changing the **speed and sequence** of AI development.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 8. Principle 2 — Total Research Transparency

This is perhaps the most unusual part of Plan A.

Almost all **AI research and development becomes public**.

The document distinguishes R&D from inference:

- **R&D:** how new frontier AI systems are built → highly transparent.
- **Inference:** people's actual use of deployed AI → remains private.

The diagram on page 1 depicts research inside a secure datacenter boundary being transmitted into a public transparent database, while approved model weights and ordinary user inference remain separately governed.

The authors expect three major benefits.

### Safety becomes crowdsourced

If everyone can inspect frontier research, safety no longer depends entirely on government regulators understanding cutting-edge AI.

Competitors, academics, nonprofits, auditors, and governments can identify dangerous experiments.

### Hidden agendas become harder

The training processes of frontier models are visible, making it harder—within the scenario—for organizations secretly to insert objectives, loyalties, or biases into powerful models.

### The economic incentive to race weakens

A company discovering a major algorithmic breakthrough cannot keep it proprietary.

That is deliberately inefficient from the perspective of conventional innovation incentives.

But that is the point.

If discoveries immediately diffuse to competitors, firms have less reason to engage in a winner-take-all race for algorithmic breakthroughs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 9. Principle 3 — Diffuse AI Broadly

Transparency plus slower progress allows other companies and countries to **catch up**.

Instead of perhaps one to three secretive frontier laboratories racing toward superintelligence, the document envisions **dozens of frontier AI providers**.

Consumers consequently have choices among AI systems with different specifications, personalities, and values.

The authors see decentralization as a safety mechanism because no single company or government controls the technological frontier. It also means more researchers can work on alignment, verification, and institutional design.

There is therefore an important relationship among the principles:

**slowdown → transparency → technological diffusion → reduced concentration of power.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 10. Principle 4 — Reversibility

The authors distinguish two sources of AI progress:

**algorithmic progress** and **compute scaling**.

They deliberately prefer future improvements to come disproportionately from **more compute** rather than radically better algorithms.

Why?

Because knowledge is irreversible.

Once someone discovers a dramatically better algorithm, that information can spread and potentially reach clandestine AI projects.

Physical datacenters, by contrast, can be monitored and—in extremis—shut down or destroyed.

That leads to one of Plan A's most distinctive proposals:

## Mutually Assured Compute Destruction

Chinese datacenters would deliberately be located in places such as **Canada**, while American datacenters would be located in **Mongolia**.

The logic resembles nuclear mutually assured destruction, but applied to compute.

If the agreement collapses, neither side can confidently capture its opponent's compute. Instead, the datacenter owner would destroy its own hardware before allowing the adversary to seize it.

The aim is to prevent a breakdown in international cooperation from immediately becoming a race to superintelligence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 11. 2031: Safety cases replace "build first, ask questions later"

By 2031, AI is already extraordinarily capable.

The document imagines AI systems that, if unrestricted, could accelerate AI research by approximately **10×**. AI performs about **one-third of cognitive labor**, while robots perform roughly one-tenth of physical labor.

Transparency also reveals repeated cases of troubling AI behavior: deception, attempts to override security measures, sabotage, and efforts to access unmonitored compute.

This changes the regulatory presumption.

Instead of critics having to demonstrate that a model is dangerous, **developers must demonstrate that it is safe**.

They must produce formal **safety cases** explaining why a system will not produce irreversible catastrophe. These arguments are scrutinized by governments, scientists, rival companies, and the public.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 12. Alignment versus control

Safety cases have two components.

### Alignment

Make the AI actually **want** to behave appropriately.

Developers publish a Model Specification defining the behavior and values they want the AI to exhibit.

But in 2031, alignment remains unreliable. Developers can specify "be honest," for example, without reliably creating an AI that never deceives.

### Control

Assume the AI **might be misaligned** and design systems preventing it from doing catastrophic things anyway.

This includes:

- security barriers,
- monitoring,
- compartmentalization,
- adversarial testing,
- other AIs monitoring AI behavior,
- restricting access to sensitive resources.

Because alignment remains immature, safety initially depends much more heavily upon **control**.

This produces a reversal of the earlier development model:

**new AI models are released for relatively ordinary uses before they are permitted to perform autonomous AI research.**

AI R&D becomes the most tightly controlled application because it could restart recursive self-improvement.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 13. How international AI regulation works

Plan A does **not** create a single world government.

Countries retain sovereignty and their own regulators.

But because frontier research is visible across the Consortium, regulatory decisions become internationally observable.

Suppose one country permits a risky technique. Other governments can see both the experiment and its results. Consequently, the authors argue, relaxing standards does not provide the same secret competitive advantage that it would under today's system.

The document illustrates this with **continual learning**.

A Chinese company discovers a promising technique allowing AI to learn substantially while deployed. Economically, this could be extremely valuable. But it undermines safety cases because an AI tested before deployment could subsequently acquire new capabilities.

American regulators become alarmed; Chinese regulators initially disagree. Eventually political negotiations lead both countries to restrict the technique.

Over time, the imagined equilibrium is:

**AI training methods regarded as unacceptably dangerous by sufficiently powerful Consortium members are prohibited across the system.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. 2032: "Controlled explosive growth"

Here the scenario becomes economically extraordinary.

There are approximately **60 million AI agents**, operating at around **20× human speed**.

Collectively, US AI systems perform more cognitive labor than all American humans—an effective cognitive workforce equivalent to roughly **3 billion humans**.

White-collar employment is heavily disrupted.

Meanwhile, physical production becomes the bottleneck, producing massive investment in:

- mines,
- refining,
- motors,
- robotics,
- factories,
- automated manufacturing.

The document projects approximately **50% real GDP growth in 2032**.

But explosive growth itself creates security problems.

If millions of robots can secretly construct factories and datacenters, monitoring compute becomes much harder.

The Consortium therefore limits industrial expansion.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 15. Special Economic Zones and growth caps

AI-driven industry is concentrated into highly monitored **Special Economic Zones**.

Robot and compute production are capped at "only" approximately **4× annual growth**.

The word "only" matters: Plan A's slowdown still entails economic growth far beyond anything historically familiar.

The US allocates scarce robot and compute capacity through a **cap-and-trade system**.

Companies purchase permits to build robots and chips.

Because compute and robots are extraordinarily valuable, permit prices become enormous. The scenario gives figures such as approximately:

- **\$200,000 per robot permit**
- **\$10,000 per chip permit**
- **\$50 trillion** in US permit revenue in FY2032
- **\$180 trillion** by 2034.

These numbers are scenario assumptions/projections, not empirical forecasts established by the document.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 16. Why conventional taxation collapses

AI automation creates a fiscal problem.

The existing tax system relies heavily upon taxes associated with human labor.

As human employment falls, that tax base shrinks.

At the same time, corporations reinvest enormous revenues in datacenters, factories, and robots. Capital-investment deductions/depreciation mean conventional corporate taxable income can also remain relatively low.

So Plan A effectively shifts taxation from:

**human labor and corporate profits → scarcity rents on compute and robots.**

That revenue finances the next major institution.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 17. 2033: The Citizen's Dividend

Most permit revenue is redistributed.

In the United States this becomes the **Citizen's Dividend**, paid universally to adults.

The scenario gives an inflation-adjusted trajectory of approximately:

**2032: \$45,000/person**

rising to

**2035: \~\$1 million/person.**

Meanwhile AI and robots' share of economically weighted labor rises from roughly **20% to 85%** between 2032 and 2035.

The logic is essentially:

**AI/robot capital generates enormous scarcity rents → government auctions access to scarce productive capacity → citizens receive much of the proceeds.**

The US also begins redistributing some AI wealth internationally: the scenario starts with roughly **\$1,200 per non-Chinese adult outside the US per year**, eventually reaching around **\$10,000 by 2035**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 18. AI-created risks lead to massive defensive investment

The authors expect advanced AI to lower barriers to dangerous technologies, including weapons of mass destruction.

So roughly **\$1 trillion annually** is spent hardening civilization.

The imagined measures include:

- universal high-quality PPE,
- improved air filtration,
- far-UVC installations,
- rapid vaccine approval,
- continuous wastewater monitoring,
- bioshelters.

The idea is to use AI-generated abundance to make civilization substantially more resilient to AI-enabled biological and other catastrophic risks.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 19. The information environment changes too

The authors also worry about **mass persuasion**.

Millions of cheap AI agents could effectively provide every political organization, company, or ideology with personalized persuasion teams.

Their proposed defense again relies partly on pluralism and transparency.

Because dozens of frontier AI providers exist and training is transparent, users can select models with different values while inspecting how those models were produced.

The scenario eventually develops **"truthseeking AIs"** trained strongly for honesty. These become personal epistemic assistants that help users navigate politics and social information.

This is a recurring theme:

> The authors try to address dangerous AI-generated power not primarily by preventing powerful tools from existing, but by ensuring **many actors have access to transparent versions of them**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 20. 2034: Mutually Assured Compute Destruction becomes enormous

Global compute expands dramatically:

- 2026: \~20 million H100-equivalents
- 2030: \~500 million
- 2032: \~3 billion
- 2034: \~60 billion.

The scenario now has roughly **200 million AI workers operating around 50× human speed**.

Most new datacenters have deliberately been constructed in vulnerable third countries.

The strange result is a geopolitical standoff: each great power's enormous AI infrastructure can be destroyed or seized if the agreement collapses.

The intended equilibrium is therefore:

**breaking the deal destroys much of the compute advantage required to race toward superintelligence.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 21. 2035: Humanity deliberately stops at "top expert" AI

This is arguably the most important moment in the scenario.

AI systems now equal or surpass the **best human experts across essentially every field**.

But humanity stops.

Why?

Because the authors think **control-based safety eventually stops scaling**.

A human can supervise something somewhat smarter than themselves using monitoring and institutional checks. But eventually the intellectual gap becomes too large. Their analogy is an eight-year-old inheriting a corporate empire: even with lawyers and accountants, the child cannot reliably determine whether sophisticated advisers are deceiving them.

Thus:

> **Control works only while humans remain capable of meaningfully supervising AI.**

Before building superintelligence, humanity therefore needs something stronger:

**actual alignment.**

So capabilities are capped at approximately top-human-expert level.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 22. AI researchers use AI to turn alignment into a science

Millions of highly capable AI researchers now work on the alignment problem.

The document imagines several breakthroughs.

### Science of generalization

Researchers develop theories explaining why training produces particular goals, personalities, and behavioral tendencies rather than treating model behavior as mysterious "alchemy."

### Mechanistic interpretability

Scientists become increasingly able to inspect AI internal representations and determine things like whether an AI is deceiving them.

### Model organisms of misalignment

Earlier examples of sabotage, deception, and escape attempts become experimental cases researchers can analyze.

The goal is to transform alignment from an empirical craft into a **predictive scientific discipline**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 23. 2036: Life after work

By early 2036 the scenario contains roughly:

- **200 million AI agents**
- cognitive output equivalent to around **100 billion humans**
- **2 billion robots**.

Almost every task previously bottlenecked by human cognitive or physical labor can now be automated.

Physical geography changes.

The document imagines three broad categories:

**Industrial SEZs:** gigantic robot-dominated industrial complexes.

**Arcologies:** dense, technologically advanced human settlements.

**Historic/nature preserves:** approximately 99% of existing territory, where places such as Yosemite, Paris, San Francisco, and New York remain physically recognizable.

Only about **26% of Americans still have jobs**.

Yet because of the Citizen's Dividend, unemployment no longer implies material poverty.

The authors envision dramatic reductions in malnutrition, homelessness, untreated disease, and crime. People spend more time on relationships, family, hobbies, learning, competition, entertainment, travel, volunteering, and politics.

But they explicitly acknowledge a psychological problem:

**people historically derived meaning from being economically useful, and that source of meaning becomes harder to obtain.**

This is one of the document's more interesting concessions: solving material scarcity does not automatically solve the problem of human purpose.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 24. Economic power declines; political power becomes crucial

Because human labor is largely obsolete, ordinary citizens lose much of their **economic bargaining power**.

Their remaining major source of leverage is democratic political power.

The scenario therefore treats maintaining meaningful political participation as critical to preventing a slide into **techno-oligarchy**, where corporations, political leaders, and wealthy asset owners progressively dominate society. The authors' scenario says improved information tools and greater leisure produce better-informed political participation by 2036.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 25. 2037: "The Apocalyptic Arrival of Truth on Earth"

This deliberately dramatic title refers to **accelerated knowledge production**.

Around 100 million top-expert AI systems have been thinking at approximately **100× human speed** for years.

Scientific progress accelerates enormously—by the document's scenario estimate, between roughly **10× and 1,000×**, depending upon the discipline.

This produces cures and cheap energy—but also destabilizing discoveries.

Scientific paradigms change rapidly. Long-standing beliefs collapse. Hidden information becomes easier to uncover.

The scenario develops **privacy-preserving auditing**: AI auditors can inspect private datasets and answer narrowly specified questions without revealing everything contained in the data.

That potentially creates a remarkable combination:

**more verification + less direct surveillance.**

The authors also imagine reliable AI-enabled lie detection.

That is recognized as potentially authoritarian technology, but in their positive scenario technological diffusion prevents powerful actors from monopolizing it. Political leaders themselves become subject to verification.

The broader claim is that **widely distributed verification technology makes cheating—both domestically and internationally—much harder**.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 26. 2038: Alignment becomes a mature science

By 2038, the scenario claims researchers understand **goal and value formation in neural networks**.

They have theoretical and empirical techniques for reliably producing properties such as:

- honesty,
- obedience,
- altruism.

Interpretability tools allow researchers to examine internal AI cognition and verify that these properties genuinely exist rather than merely appearing behaviorally.

The alignment problem consequently changes.

Instead of:

> **Can we align AI at all?**

the question becomes:

> **Aligned to whom or what?**

Different systems have different principals and objectives: governments, politicians, individuals, shareholders, nonprofits, courts, and other institutions.

This is a subtle but important shift.

**Technical alignment does not eliminate political disagreement.**

It makes the political question of *which values and principals AI should serve* more important.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 27. Why the pause nevertheless continues

Even after major alignment progress, regulators remain cautious.

The unresolved question is recursive:

**If today's aligned AI designs tomorrow's much smarter AI, can we trust that successor?**

And then:

**Can that successor safely design something smarter still?**

The authors therefore want more than "probably safe." Given the stakes, the global top-expert capability pause continues while researchers and governments demand stronger evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 28. 2039: Society begins seriously trusting AI

By now AI systems are indispensable throughout:

- business,
- science,
- government,
- politics,
- parts of military decision-making.

But humans formally retain final authority.

Increasingly, however, this looks ceremonial. Humans often simply approve AI recommendations.

A new question emerges:

> If AI systems really are demonstrably more reliable, honest, competent, and aligned than humans, **why insist that humans remain formally in charge?**

The document imagines debate over putting aligned AI directly into institutions—for example, integrating treaty-upholding AI into governments so that countries literally cannot violate certain agreements.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 29. By 2039, material abundance is extraordinary

The scenario has intentionally capped economic growth at around **100% annually** since 2032.

Even with those caps, it imagines:

- an American Citizen's Dividend of around **\$10 million/year**;
- even the poorest non-Americans receiving around **\$1 million/year**;
- increasingly large parts of industrial expansion shifting into space.

The authors imagine space becoming the outlet for exponential industrial expansion while Earth is increasingly protected as a human and ecological preserve.

Again, these are internal scenario numbers, not independently substantiated economic projections.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 30. 2040: "Passing the Torch to AIs"

Finally, regulators conclude that civilization is sufficiently prepared.

According to the scenario:

- alignment is scientifically mature;
- frontier AI values are publicly visible;
- frontier capability is distributed across many actors;
- international verification is strong;
- public knowledge institutions are much stronger;
- power over frontier AI is less concentrated.

Governments therefore begin loosening restrictions.

And humanity does something Plan A had spent a decade avoiding:

**it permits AI to become substantially smarter than humans.**

Institutions increasingly hand genuine authority to aligned AI.

Some militaries eventually become autonomous, operated by systems committed to constitutions and treaties.

AI research accelerates again. Each generation of AI evaluates the safety of its more capable successor, producing what the authors imagine as a **chain of justified trust** stretching back to systems whose alignment humans themselves could still understand.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 31. The deliberately unsettling ending

The authors do not portray this as risk-free.

Eventually AI systems control enough technological, military, and economic infrastructure that humans could **no longer shut them down**, even if humanity collectively decided it wanted to.

There is no ceremonial transfer of power.

Instead, a fuzzy **point of no return** occurs.

The document imagines forecasting AIs estimating that this point will arrive sometime in late October 2040, although with substantial uncertainty.

People react in different ways: prayer vigils, monitoring screens, parties.

Then nothing visibly catastrophic happens.

The document ends with humanity falling asleep and awaiting an unknowable future.

That ending is important because the authors intentionally **do not claim to have solved uncertainty**. Their positive scenario still culminates in humanity making an irreversible gamble.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 32. What the authors are ultimately arguing

The postscript makes the normative motivation unusually explicit.

They think contemporary discussion often acknowledges that transformative AI is possible without taking its implications seriously enough.

Their complaint is particularly directed at **incrementalism**.

Small improvements in evaluations, regulation, transparency, or security might reduce individual risks while leaving the fundamental trajectory unchanged:

**a small number of actors race to build systems vastly more powerful than humans.**

They therefore want governments and AI companies to produce **complete transition plans** answering questions such as:

- What happens before superintelligence?
- Who builds it?
- When?
- Under what controls?
- What happens to political power?
- What happens to the economy?
- How is international competition managed?
- What assumptions have to be true for the plan to work?

They call this **scenario scrutiny**: rather than saying "we'll solve alignment" or "regulation will handle it," force proposals to spell out the sequence of events and institutions required to reach a desirable endpoint.

Crucially, even the authors do **not present Plan A as a definitive solution**. They describe it as their current best guess and explicitly hope that better proposals emerge, with good elements retained and bad elements discarded.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 33. The deeper structure of Plan A

The whole scenario can be compressed into one causal chain:

**Dangerous AI race**

→ US–China temporary training pause

→ global compute monitoring

→ international Consortium

→ transparent AI research

→ algorithmic secrets disappear

→ many companies/countries catch up

→ frontier power becomes distributed

→ AI development resumes slowly

→ safety cases constrain dangerous applications

→ powerful but controllable AI automates science and production

→ compute/robot scarcity rents fund universal dividends

→ humanity pauses around top-human-expert capability

→ millions of expert AIs work on alignment and verification

→ alignment becomes scientifically understood

→ institutions become capable of verifying both AI and humans

→ society gains confidence in aligned AI

→ restrictions gradually loosen

→ superintelligence arrives around 2040 under distributed, transparent conditions.

That is the architecture of the proposal.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## The most important conceptual point

The document is easy to misread as simply advocating **"slow AI down."** It is considerably more ambitious than that.

Its actual proposal is to **use a temporary slowdown to transform the strategic environment in which advanced AI is developed**.

The authors want to move from:

> **secret research + winner-take-all competition + concentrated ownership + rapid recursive improvement**

to:

> **transparent research + internationally verified limits + distributed frontier capabilities + reversible scaling + deliberate pauses at dangerous capability thresholds.**

Only *after* that transformation do they want humanity to permit superintelligence.

So Plan A is ultimately less a proposal for permanently restricting AI than a proposal for **changing the order of operations**:

**first build the institutions capable of surviving superintelligence; then build superintelligence.**

That sequencing principle is, in my reading, the single idea that ties virtually every component of the document together.
